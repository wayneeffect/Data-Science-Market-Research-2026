**Entry-level jobs in data science** are accessible but competitive in 2026. True "pure" entry-level data scientist roles (with no prior experience) are rarer than related positions like data analyst or junior roles, as many companies prefer candidates with some practical experience, internships, or strong projects.

### Common Entry-Level Job Titles
Here are the most typical starting points:

- **Data Analyst** (most common gateway): Focus on querying data (SQL), creating dashboards (Tableau/Power BI), cleaning data, and generating business insights/reports. Often the easiest entry point.
- **Junior Data Scientist**: Basic modeling, exploratory analysis, statistical testing, and supporting senior team members on predictive tasks.
- **Junior Data Engineer / Analytics Engineer**: Building and maintaining data pipelines, databases, and ETL processes.
- **Junior Machine Learning Engineer** (more competitive): Assisting with model building, deployment, and basic AI tasks.
- **Business Intelligence (BI) Analyst** or **Analytics Consultant**: Similar to data analyst but often more business-oriented.
- **Data Science Intern / Fellow / Associate** (great for new grads): Many full-time roles start here.

Other titles you’ll see: Associate Data Scientist, Entry-Level Data Scientist, or hybrid roles combining data with software development.

### Typical Requirements
- **Education**: Bachelor’s degree in data science, statistics, computer science, math, or a related field. Master’s or PhD can help for pure data scientist roles but isn’t always required for analyst positions.
- **Skills**:
  - Core: SQL, Python (or R), Excel/Google Sheets, data visualization (Tableau, Power BI, Matplotlib).
  - Nice-to-have: Statistics/probability, basic machine learning (scikit-learn), cloud (AWS/GCP/Azure), Git.
  - Soft skills: Communication, business acumen, problem-solving.
- **Experience**: Internships, capstone projects, personal portfolios (Kaggle, GitHub), or bootcamps are often essential to stand out. Pure zero-experience roles are limited.

### Salaries (US, Approximate 2026 Figures)
Salaries vary significantly by location (tech hubs higher), company, and exact role:

- **Data Analyst (entry-level)**: $65,000–$90,000 (sometimes up to $110k+ in high-cost areas).
- **Junior Data Scientist**: $85,000–$120,000+ (median around $95k–$112k).
- **Junior ML Engineer**: $60,000–$110,000 range.
- Total compensation can include bonuses and stock (especially at tech firms).

Higher pay is common in finance, tech, healthcare, and consulting. Remote roles exist but are competitive.

### Job Market Reality in 2026
- **Demand is strong overall**, but entry-level is tougher due to high supply of graduates/bootcamp alumni and companies raising the bar (expecting production-ready skills or domain knowledge).
- Data analyst roles are generally more accessible than pure data scientist positions.
- AI skills (e.g., basic LLMs, prompt engineering) are increasingly valued even at junior levels.
- Growth outlook remains excellent long-term (BLS projects strong expansion).

### Tips to Land One
- Build a strong portfolio showcasing end-to-end projects (data cleaning → analysis → insights → visualization).
- Gain experience through internships, freelance, open-source, or personal projects.
- Network on LinkedIn, contribute to Kaggle competitions, and tailor applications.
- Consider starting in data analysis or adjacent roles and transitioning upward.
- Certifications (Google Data Analytics, Microsoft, etc.) can help but projects matter more.

**Bottom line**: Start with data analyst or junior analyst roles as a realistic entry, then move into data science. The field rewards practical skills, curiosity, and demonstrated impact over perfect credentials. Persistence and a solid portfolio are key in this competitive but rewarding market. Check sites like Indeed, LinkedIn, Built In, or company career pages for current openings.
