**Mid-level data science jobs** (typically 3–6 years of relevant experience) represent a significant step up from entry-level roles. You move from supporting projects to owning them end-to-end, with greater autonomy, impact, and often some mentoring responsibilities.

### Common Mid-Level Job Titles
- **Data Scientist** (standard mid-level title)
- **Senior Data Scientist** (sometimes used for the upper end of mid-level)
- **Machine Learning Engineer** (hybrid or specialized)
- **Analytics Engineer** or **Senior Analytics Engineer**
- **Quantitative Analyst** (especially in finance)
- Other variations: Lead Data Scientist (smaller teams), ML Ops Engineer, or domain-specific titles (e.g., Healthcare Data Scientist)

### Typical Responsibilities
Mid-level professionals are expected to handle more complex, business-critical work independently:
- Lead end-to-end projects (problem framing → data collection/cleaning → modeling → deployment → monitoring).
- Build and deploy predictive/prescriptive models (e.g., customer churn, recommendation systems, forecasting).
- Design and run experiments (A/B testing, causal inference).
- Mentor juniors and collaborate with cross-functional teams (engineering, product, business stakeholders).
- Translate technical results into actionable business recommendations.
- Improve data pipelines, implement MLOps practices, and ensure model scalability/reliability.

### Requirements
- **Experience**: Usually 3–6 years in data science/analytics roles (or strong equivalent from adjacent fields like software engineering or statistics).
- **Education**: Bachelor’s degree minimum; Master’s in data science, statistics, computer science, or related field is common and often preferred.
- **Key Skills**:
  - Advanced Python/R, SQL.
  - Machine learning frameworks (scikit-learn, TensorFlow/PyTorch, XGBoost).
  - Cloud platforms (AWS, GCP, Azure) and deployment tools.
  - Statistics, experimentation, and data storytelling.
  - Version control (Git), containerization (Docker), and orchestration basics.
- **Differentiators**: Production experience, business impact examples, and emerging AI/LLM skills (e.g., RAG, fine-tuning) are highly valued in 2026.

### Salaries (US, 2026 Estimates)
Salaries vary by location (tech hubs like SF/NY pay more), industry (finance/tech highest), and company size. Figures often include base + bonus/stock:

- **Typical range**: $115,000 – $175,000 total compensation.
- Common mid-level benchmarks: $138k–$175k nationally; some reports show $141k–$180k for remote or strong performers.
- Glassdoor-style medians around $140k–$160k total pay, with top end exceeding $190k in competitive markets.
- Higher in specialized areas (e.g., AI/ML-focused or big tech).

### Market Outlook
Demand remains strong for mid-level talent because companies value proven delivery over pure potential. The field continues to grow (BLS projects fast expansion), but success depends on demonstrable impact and adaptability to AI advancements. Hybrid roles blending data science with engineering or domain expertise are especially sought after.

### Transition Tips from Entry-Level
- Focus on owning full project lifecycles and quantifying your impact (e.g., “Improved revenue by X% via model”).
- Build production skills (deployment, monitoring, scalability).
- Develop communication and leadership abilities.
- Contribute to open source, publish blog posts, or speak internally.
- Network and target internal promotions or lateral moves into more technical teams.

**Bottom line**: Mid-level data science roles offer substantial responsibility, impact, and compensation. They reward professionals who combine technical depth with business acumen and reliability. In 2026, those who can ship robust, production-ready solutions—especially with modern AI tools—stand out the most. Check LinkedIn, Indeed, or company career pages for current openings, and tailor your resume to highlight ownership and results.
